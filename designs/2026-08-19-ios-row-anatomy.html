The row, settled

Three elements on the row, everything else moved to a gesture or to the chrome. Each control has one job, and the two that sit under a scrolling thumb are both undone by repeating the tap.

The two swipes never collide: they run in opposite directions and start in different places — the back gesture from the screen's left edge, the zoom reveal from anywhere on the row.

3 · Swipe to zoom, in both its states

One gesture, two ways through. A shallow swipe parks the button and waits for a tap. Pushing past roughly half the row's width stretches it full-width and fires a medium impact haptic — the tick says releasing now will commit, so you can back off before letting go.

4 · How far is far enough

Distance is the smaller half of the answer. What actually stops an accidental swipe is the angle: iOS decides early whether a pan is horizontal or vertical, and a thumb scrolling a list moves mostly vertically. Requiring the gesture to be more horizontal than vertical before the row wins over the scroll is the real guard — with that in place the distances below are comfortable rather than load-bearing.

What has to change for touch

  • Arming. Vertical pan means scroll, so the drag needs a long press plus a haptic tick before it takes over
  • Occlusion. The finger covers the row it targets, so the insertion line runs full-width and the lifted row rides above the touch point
  • Depth precision. An 18px indent step is finer than a fingertip; either widen the step on mobile or snap with a haptic at each level
  • Portable already. dropTarget.ts is pure — tree, rows and pointer in, a target out — so it ports to AnsaCore as arithmetic, not as interaction

6 · The chrome around the list

The breadcrumb and bar stay at the top but scroll away with the list — they describe where you are, which you read on arrival rather than continuously. Only capture is fixed, and it moves to the bottom: it satisfies the original "reachable without scrolling" reason just as well as the top did, while sitting in the thumb's arc and rising with the keyboard instead of jumping across the screen.

Open questions

  • Only one fixed element now. With the header scrolling away, the list keeps its height and the keyboard only has the capture bar to push. That removes the squeeze without needing a hide-while-typing rule.
  • The disc carries more now. With the chevron gone and the count gone, a solid disc is the only thing saying children are hidden. True on the web too, but the phone has no hover and narrower rows. If it reads too quietly, make the collapsed state more emphatic rather than bringing the number back.
  • Depth without a right-hand marker. Indent is carried by the row's left padding and the nesting guides, which become load-bearing rather than decorative.
