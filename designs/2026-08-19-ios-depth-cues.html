Depth without guides

Four ways to say how deep a row sits, with the real tutorial tree and the app's own tokens. Guides are shown first as the baseline they'd replace — they break between rows because each row draws its own segment.

A · Guides (today)

One hairline per ancestor. Precise, and the only one that stays readable past three levels. Dashes wherever a row boundary falls.

B · Surface tint

Depth shifts the row's background. Survives wrapping, costs no horizontal space, needs no lines. Collapses past ~3 levels.

C · Leading bar

One continuous 3pt bar per row instead of N hairlines, colour deepening with level. Reads as one object, not a dotted column.

D · Type weight

Level 0 semibold, level 1 regular, level 2 smaller and dimmer. Zero chrome. Fails when a deep row is alone on screen.

What the phone screenshot actually showed: rows wrap to three lines, so the text's left edge moves more from wrapping than from nesting. Any cue that lives in the horizontal gap is fighting that. B and C don't — they mark the row, not the space beside it.

  • B is the safest and the cheapest to build. Even once the tints stop being separable, the boundary between two rows still says which is deeper.
  • C keeps more precision than B and doesn't dash, but adds a coloured element to a row that already has two marks on its left.
  • D is free and pairs with either — worth stacking on B rather than shipping alone.

None of these fix deep trees. That's zoom's job, and Workflowy's own mobile app answers it that way: it barely renders depth because you're never more than a level or two from your scope.
